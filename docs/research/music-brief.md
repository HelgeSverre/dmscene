# Dmscene theme research brief

## Objective
Improve ONE VM / NO SLEEP: an original 51.2-second tracker/synth soundtrack for a self-showcasing terminal demo. The existing track is technically functional but musically repetitive and unconvincing. Learn practical composition, groove, sound-selection and arrangement techniques from first-person forum discussions and demoscene references, then implement a fresh score within the existing C99 engine.

## Decision questions
1. How can a short, memorable motif develop across eight four-bar visual chapters?
2. How should kick, bass, percussion and syncopation share rhythm and frequency space?
3. Which changes create a convincing breakdown, build and payoff within 32 bars?
4. How can tracker constraints and synthesized instruments create character rather than a generic wall of sound?
5. Which demoscene works demonstrate relevant pacing and audiovisual relationships, without copying their music or visuals?
6. How can we verify the new score's note gates, headroom, section contrast and render integrity?

## Evidence priorities
First-person forum discussion and primary production pages first; follow relevant original descriptions/credits where available. Avoid generic SEO advice. Forum advice is practitioner opinion, not an objective rule. A production page can establish credits and availability, but cannot alone prove its audiovisual contents: mark observation versus inference.

## Report structure for each track
- Scope and sources actually read.
- 4–6 actionable findings, each linked to its supporting source and marked high/medium/low confidence.
- Contradictions and limitations.
- Specific applications to the existing .dmt format and 32-bar theme.
- For creative references: explain what could inspire the technique and what must remain original.

## Synthesis and implementation
Compare convergence/divergence across the three tracks; produce a concise source-linked research note and original arrangement plan. Rewrite the score, preserve the eight chapter boundaries and CLI tour, regenerate MP4/WAV/GIF, and validate note releases, deterministic audio, level/headroom and section dynamics. Subjective quality still requires the user's listening judgment.
